      What Depth Wizard does — and what it doesn’t

      Depth Wizard turns satellite imagery into an interactive 3D terrain view. The terrain you see is always a real digital elevation model (DEM). Satellite imagery is draped on it as texture, and the output of a depth model (Depth Anything V2) is shown only as a labelled relative-depth visualisation — never as measured height.

      The three pages

      1

      DW Studio

      • Choose from Library: 88 curated real scenes (6 Maxar VHR, 50 DFC2019, 32 Sentinel-2) with their source, ground resolution and location.
      • Upload a GeoTIFF, PNG or JPG. A GeoTIFF’s ground resolution is read from its geotransform: at 2.4 m/pixel or finer it is routed to Tier 2 (height prediction) and you upload a DEM or have FABDEM fetched; coarser goes to Tier 1 (FABDEM terrain only, fetched automatically). PNG/JPG has no location, so only the relative preview applies.
      • Search Online queries the Copernicus Data Space Ecosystem live for Sentinel-2 L2A scenes (always Tier 1, FABDEM fetched automatically). It shows the account’s documented quota and this app’s own usage, and you can use your own Copernicus key.
      • The Start generation button is green for Tier 2, orange for Tier 1.
      • Each start is a job. Generate New starts another without closing the current one. The jobs panel lists them newest first, and the 3D view has a tab per job. Each job can be saved, pinned or deleted. Save downloads the job as JSON and keeps a copy under Saved in this browser, where it can be reopened later. Unsaved jobs live only in this tab, and leaving with any asks first. The panel icon hides or shows the jobs panel.
      • Start generation generates from your scene: relative depth (Depth Anything V2 Small) from the image itself, and elevation, the DSM and the 3D terrain from a real elevation model for its footprint: FABDEM and Copernicus GLO-30 for library Sentinel-2 tiles, a DEM you attach, or GLO-30 fetched live. Height is never inferred from the image. A scene without a georeference (DFC2019 tiles, plain PNG/JPG) has no elevation, and the panels say so.

      Where the heights come from

      Every 3D surface in the app is a DEM. The three plains regions use Copernicus GLO-30 at 30 m, so they read less detailed than Darjeeling — that is the data, not a bug.

      RegionTerrain sourceElevation rangeLandscape
      DarjeelingOpenTopography DSM (Copernicus GLO-30)557–2478 mhilly, forested
      KolkataCopernicus GLO-30−8–35 murban
      BardhamanCopernicus GLO-3018–50 magricultural
      SundarbansCopernicus GLO-300–10 mcoastal, mangrove

      Relative depth is frozen Depth Anything V2 (ViT-Large). It orders pixels near-to-far; it has no metres. On nadir satellite imagery it does not reliably track elevation: on Darjeeling its correlation with the DEM flipped from +0.60 to −0.41 once the broad spatial trend was removed.

      What the research found

      Alongside the app, a research track tested whether real metric height can be predicted from imagery. The short answer depends on resolution.

      SENTINEL-2 · 10 m · IN THE APP

      DEM only

      Across 32 Indian test tiles, nothing tested on top of the imagery (frequency fusion, random-forest and linear residuals, CNN corrections, a fine-tuned depth model, a ranking loss) beat simply using the DEM. The best terrain DEM is FABDEM — median error 1.78 m against ICESat-2 ground photons — and the best surface DEM is Copernicus GLO-30 (4.48 m median against canopy-top segments). A second coarse sensor (Landsat, 15 m) behaved the same way.

      VERY HIGH RESOLUTION · ~0.3 m · RESEARCH ONLY

      Predicted height, gated on imagery

      On 0.3 m imagery (the DFC2019 benchmark) a fine-tuned Depth Anything V2 does beat a strong per-tile baseline (MAE 1.98 m, RMSE 3.49 m). An offline pipeline builds DSM = FABDEM + predicted height above ground and was run on 6 Maxar crops in Sikkim and Darjeeling. In the app it is used only as the surface model of those 6 Maxar library crops (precomputed, labelled as a research model). It under-states tall canopy (a ceiling around 18–23 m), it did not generalise to US aerial imagery, and it has only been tested on mountain terrain.

      In synthetic tests, a model trained on imagery coarsened to 5 m still clearly beat the baseline; from 6 m on it no longer did. That is why learned height is tied to very-high-resolution imagery and not to Sentinel-2.
